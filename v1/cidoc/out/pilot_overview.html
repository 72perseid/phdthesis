Pilot on the 45th Kazı Sonuçları Toplantısı volume

From an excavation report to data that can be asked questions

An excavator fills familiar tables. The tables only accept what CIDOC CRM allows. The knowledge graph is generated from them by fixed rules. This page follows one paragraph of one report through every step.

Step 1 · Report

One paragraph about one oven

Yumuktepe Höyük, 2024 season. The paragraph holds seven separate facts. In the printed volume they can only be found by reading.

A large part of the horseshoe-shaped, stone-founded oven called BX lies inside the north section. At the hearth level, Beirut-made Brittle Ware and 13th-century glazed and incised pottery of Port St. Simeon origin were recovered (G. Köroğlu, personal communication). Barley taken from the BX oven, which was destroyed by pit B2ç1, gave a calibrated radiocarbon range of AD 1387 to 1476.

What the tables refuse

The database checks every entry at the moment it is made. Twelve kinds of wrong entry were tried. All twelve were refused. Some of them:

  • A find from a trench that does not exist
  • A find with no unit that found it
  • A class that is not in the ontologies
  • A find recorded as a person
  • A period that ends before it begins
  • A context that cuts itself
  • A relation type nobody defined
  • A field the tables have no column for
Step 3 · Graph

The rows as a CIDOC CRM graph

A program writes the graph from the tables. The same column always gives the same path, so graphs from different excavations can be queried together. The drawing shows the part that comes from the paragraph above, simplified.

The pottery date is stored as a claim by a named person. The radiocarbon range is stored as a laboratory observation. The graph keeps them apart, so a reader can always tell what was measured from what was judged.

Next

What comes after the pilot

  • Load the remaining 23 papers of the volume. A language model fills the tables and a person checks them. The model never chooses classes.
  • Count how many table changes each new paper forces. That number is the test of the design. The third paper forced four new tables and three new columns.
  • Add coordinates and geometry, which no table holds yet.
  • Move from reports to real excavation files, starting with one excavation database.
Limits

What this does not show yet

  • A report is a summary of an excavation. It is not the excavation's data. The pilot shows the method, not the full record.
  • Three papers are too few to claim the tables are complete.
  • People, places and periods are not yet matched across papers.
  • Web addresses in the graph are placeholders.
